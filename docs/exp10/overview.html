Exp 10 · Broken input, photoreal output, skeleton as control

Overview

The first experiment with a genuinely broken input. Take a flawed text-to-motion clip, keep its skeleton on only K frames, and let the video model redraw the bad frames in between.

The goal#

Experiments 6–9 used clean motion capture as the input. Here the input is MotionGPT3's output for a text prompt, with all of its natural errors.

The idea is that sparsity does not improve the frames that are kept. It throws away the bad frames in between and lets the video prior redraw them. So the question is whether what comes back is a better motion than what went in.

Setup#

  • Input: MotionGPT3 (text-to-motion), fitted to SMPL, 169 frames @ 25 fps. Twelve prompts in three tiers: easy, medium, hard.
  • Control: a DWPose skeleton on the K anchor frames, black elsewhere.
  • Video model: LTX-2.3 22B distilled + Union-Control IC-LoRA, 768×768.
  • Recovery: GVHMR, re-rendered on a neutral SMPL body.
  • Blend baseline: straight-line interpolation between the same anchors, with no model.

What came out#

What we observed
  • Refinement in 8 of 12 motions, most often at K = 5. sit_down, stand_up, wave and walk improve at every K.
  • The chair. In the input, sit_down and stand_up sit on nothing. The video puts a real chair under the person at every K.
  • The model beats the blend on jerk and foot skate at every K, and departs from the input smoothly as K drops. There is no sudden cliff.
  • jump is a sampling failure. The anchors miss its airborne frame, so the jump disappears. run turns into a walk, and throw becomes a baseball swing with an invented bat.
