Exp 15 · Broken input — bad, uniform and hand-picked keyframes

Overview

Exps 11 and 12 found that automatic keyframe choice rarely mattered. Test the best case and the worst case directly, with keyframes picked by hand and keyframes picked to be bad. Then ask whether a stage-by-stage prompt can act as control on its own.

The goal#

If keyframe choice matters at all, hand-picked keyframes should beat evenly spaced ones, and deliberately bad picks (uninformative or broken poses) should be worse. All picks were made in a picker tool and frozen before any video was generated.

The second question is about text. A staged prompt ("first…, then…, then…") is tried on its own and together with keyframes. The idea being tested is that text supplies meaning while keyframes supply timing.

Setup#

  • Input: MotionGPT3 (flawed) on twelve prompts in tiers: simple, moderate, complex, plus three added later. Matching the input is not the goal.
  • Control: five keyframes (0 and 168 always included), drawn two ways:
  • black: the full skeleton on the 5 keyframes, black between.
  • trunk: trunk plus wrist and ankle dots on every frame, full skeleton on the 5 keyframes.
  • Video model: LTX-2.3 22B distilled + Union-Control IC-LoRA, 768×768. Three seeds. Recovery with GVHMR.

What came out#

What we observed
  • Hand-picked does not beat uniform. They tie in every tier. With trunk control there is no ordering at all: bad picks do as well as hand-picked ones.
  • Bad keyframes hurt only with black between, and only where the prompt doesn't already name the action.
  • Staged prompt + hand-picked + trunk is the only condition that does every stage in order on all 3 seeds of walk_wave and walk_stop_turn.
  • Some prompts fail everywhere. run_jump never jumps (0 of 27 clips), the parrot is never petted, and kneel_pick_stand squats rather than kneels in 22 of 27, because the keyframes override the word "kneel".
  • The trunk halves foot skate (229 → ~100 mm/s).
